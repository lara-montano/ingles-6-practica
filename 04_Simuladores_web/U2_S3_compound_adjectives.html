💡 How it works

Two words, one adjective

English compresses a whole description into a two-word adjective joined by a hyphen: a hotel that welcomes families → a family-friendly hotel. Five patterns do most of the work in travel writing. (Cinco patrones productivos; el guion une las dos piezas.)

    The singular rule — never “two-hours”

    In a number compound the number already says how many, so the noun inside stays singular. (El sustantivo dentro del compuesto va en singular.)

    ✘ a two-hours delay
    ✔ a two-hour delay
    ✘ a three-weeks vacation
    ✔ a three-week vacation

    Outside the compound the plural comes back: The delay lasted two hours. · More: a five-star resort · a ten-hour flight · an 18-year-old traveler

    📍 When the hyphen disappears

    Position matters: before the noun vs after be

    Pick a compound, then move it. Before a noun you hyphenate; after be an adverb + past participle compound usually drops the hyphen, while a noun + adjective compound keeps it. (La posición decide el guion.)

    And never over-hyphenate: compounds that dictionaries list as one word stay solid — shortsighted, sunburned, waterproof.

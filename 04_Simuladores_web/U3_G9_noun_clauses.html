💡 How it works

A whole clause in a noun’s slot

A noun clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb that works like a single noun. Wherever a noun can go — subject, object or after a preposition — a noun clause can go too. It begins with that, a wh- word (what, who, which, when, where, why, how) or if / whether. Pick a clause type and a slot, and watch the clause slide in. (Toda la cláusula ocupa el lugar de un sustantivo.)

    💡 The golden rule

    Statement order inside the clause

    Inside a noun clause use statement word order (subject before verb). Do not use do/does/did and do not invert, even when the clause reports a question — the same rule as reported questions (Grammar 7). Pick a question, look at the broken clause, then fix it. (Dentro de la cláusula: orden de afirmación, sin do/does.)

    ✨ The spotlight pattern

    “What we need is …”

    Put a what-clause in the subject slot to spotlight the important part. The clause counts as one thing, so the verb is singular. («Lo que necesitamos es…»: lo que = what, nunca «the what».)

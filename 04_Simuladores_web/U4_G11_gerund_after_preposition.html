💡 How it works

After any preposition, the verb takes -ing

The rule is simple: after any preposition, a verb takes the gerund (the -ing form) — never the infinitive. Prepositions attach to verbs, adjectives and nouns, or come as short words of their own. Pick a head word on the dial and watch the machine fill the slots. (Tras una preposición, el verbo va en -ing: interested in working, no “in to work”.)

💡 The two “to”s

Infinitive to or preposition to?

to + base form = infinitiveI want to go. · She decided to wear the red dress. · I’d like to try it on.
to + -ing = to is a prepositionI look forward to going. · I’m not used to wearing a suit. · Staff object to wearing uniforms.

The test: if you can replace “to + verb” with “to it”, then to is a preposition → use -ing. Tap a sentence and run the test. (Si puedes decir “to it”, ese to es preposición: -ing.)

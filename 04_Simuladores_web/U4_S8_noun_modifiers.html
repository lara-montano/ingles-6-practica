💡 How it works

Stack the nouns: the head goes last

English piles nouns up to describe a product in a few words. The last noun is the head — the main thing; every noun to its left describes the one on its right. Read a long chain from right to left. (El núcleo va al final; se lee de derecha a izquierda.)

⚠️ The singular rule

The modifier noun is singular

The noun that describes works like an adjective, so it drops the plural -s — even when the idea is plural: a two-door car, never two-doors. Tap a pair to see it before and after. (El sustantivo que modifica va en singular.)

🗜️ The compressor

From the long of / for / that version to the chain

Both versions are correct English. The long one explains; the compressed one is what labels, adverts and product names use. Pick a phrase and compress it. (La versión larga explica; la comprimida es la de las etiquetas y anuncios.)

🚑 Typical mistakes

What Spanish speakers usually get wrong

In Spanish the head comes first and the modifier follows with de (botella de agua); in English the order is reversed and the head lands at the end. (Invierte el orden del español y lee la cadena de derecha a izquierda.)

💡 How it works

The comma switch: defining vs non-defining

A relative clause gives more information about a noun, joining two ideas into one smoother sentence. A defining clause identifies which one — essential information, no commas. A non-defining clause adds extra information about something already identified — always inside commas, and never with that. Flip the switch and watch the meaning change. (Sin comas = identifica cuál; con comas = solo añade un dato.)

✂️ Can I omit the pronoun?

Drop it only when it is the object (defining clauses)

Choose a phrase, then try to drop the pronoun. Quick test: is there a subject (we, they, I, a noun) right after the pronoun? Yes → the pronoun is the object → optional. No → it is the subject → keep it. Whose is never dropped. (Solo se omite el pronombre objeto, y solo sin comas.)

💡 How it works

Looking forward from a point in the past

Sometimes we talk about something that was still in the future at an earlier moment — a plan, an intention or an obligation we had then. English marks this with special future-in-the-past forms, and they very often describe things that didn't happen. Pick a form, then choose the outcome and watch the ending change. (El futuro visto desde un punto del pasado: los planes, obligaciones y promesas de entonces.)

🔁 The hindsight dial

Then vs now: the same future, one step back

A future form seen from the past moves one step back, exactly as in reported speech (Grammar 7): will → would, am going to → was going to. Time words move too: tonight → that night, tomorrow → the next day. (La forma de futuro retrocede un paso; también las palabras de tiempo.)
